Editorial Policy

How we choose topics, and how we write.

Topics

A topic must rest on solid historical records or classic texts; rumours and unverified claims are avoided. The same topic is not written twice.

Verification

Key dates, people, places and figures are cross-checked against public sources during writing, favouring primary documents, academic publications and reputable institutions. Wikipedia is a starting point for verification, never the sole source.

Distinctions

Historical fact, scholarly inference and authorial opinion are kept apart in the text. Where scholars disagree on a figure, the differing accounts are presented together rather than pretending one number settles it.

Prohibited

No invented historical stories, no fabricated references, and no passing off rewritten content from other sites as new articles.

Honest dates

Publication dates are recorded exactly as they are — never backdated or postdated. When an article is corrected or updated, only the last-updated date changes; the publication date stays as it was.

The role of AI

This site uses AI to assist with drafting and translation, but every article is verified and reviewed by a human editor. AI output is not the finished product — verification is.

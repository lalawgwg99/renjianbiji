    The Wushe Incident, 1930: An Uprising in the Mountains, and the Price of a People

    On October 27, 1930, six Seediq villages rose up at the athletics meet of the Wushe Public School; within two months, about 640 of the roughly 1,200 people who took part were dead. The following spring, the survivors were attacked again and forced to relocate to Chuanzhongdao. This essay asks: why would a people pushed to the edge choose a road they knew led nowhere — and how did one uprising rewrite Taiwan's colonial history and the fate of its Indigenous peoples?

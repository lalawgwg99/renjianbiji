Bestseller Deep Reads

Across all themes, chosen from the charts: bestselling books with real substance, one deep read per book.

  • Classics & Ideas · About 12 min read

    The Almanack of Naval Ravikant: How a Free Book Taught the World to Get Rich and Be Happy

    From a tweet thread to the top of Taiwan's bestseller charts — Naval's lessons in wealth and happiness

  • Taiwan · About 9 min read

    Taiwan Travelogue: A Novel Pretending to Be a Translation, and How the World Learned to Read Taiwan

    In 1938, two women sharing a single name board a train across Taiwan and eat their way through twelve dishes; in 2026, the book won the International Booker Prize, the first Chinese-language work ever to do so.

  • Science & the World · About 9 min read

    Rewire: Your Brain Is Not Concrete — It Is a Tangle of Wires That Can Be Reconnected

    A 2024 British neuroscience bestseller won Taiwan over with one sentence: your brain can be rewired. This deep read takes apart its three-phase programme — and asks how much of it is actually new.

  • People & Society · About 8 min read

    Counselling for Toads: What Ten Sessions with a Depressed Toad Can Teach Us

    A 1997 British book uses a fairy-tale toad to act out psychotherapy; it sold millions against the tide of a pandemic — because it turned "what is wrong with me" into a story you can talk about.

  • People & Society · About 9 min read

    Atomic Habits: 1% Better Every Day, 37 Times Better in a Year — Is That Really True?

    A 2018 book on habits sold 25 million copies on the strength of one equation. It shrank change into a two-minute daily act — and dressed arithmetic up as science. This deep read takes its most famous formula apart.

  • People & Society · About 9 min read

    The Let Them Theory: How a Four-Word Book Sold Ten Million Copies

    Four words, ten million copies, a podcast empire — and a 33-point evidence review. This book is the century’s best-selling reminder, and a method well worth taking apart.

  • Money & Human Nature · About 12 min read

    The Forrest Gump Way of Investing: Execution — A Straight Road Walked for Ten Years. Why Do Most People Fail to Finish It?

    From "don't watch the market, don't pick stocks, don't time your buys" to "switching on the excess-return engine" — Chueh Yu-shang splits ten years of Forrest Gump investing into a planning volume and an execution volume. This deep read does not repeat the method; it asks three questions: is the straight road really straight? What does "earning a little extra" cost? And which numbers in the marketing can be checked, and which cannot?

  • Money & Human Nature · About 10 min read

    The Thinking Machine: From a Denny's Booth to Five Trillion Dollars — and a Driver Change at the Top

    Published in April 2025, Stephen Witt's Nvidia biography won the FT and Schroders Business Book of the Year. Nineteen months later, its subject resigned as chief executive. This deep read does not chase gossip; it asks what Jensen Huang got right — and which of the very things he got right came back to bite him in November 2026.

  • Money & Human Nature · About 11 min read

    The Secret History of Gold: A 6,700-Year-Old Piece of Gold — Why Is It Still Rising Today?

    Published in August 2025, this history of gold covers 5,000 years of monetary history in 16 chapters, all to prove one claim: gold does not rise; everything else falls against it. This deep read asks whether that claim is true — or only a story a gold bull tells himself.

  • People & Society · About 10 min read

    The Why Cafe: Three Questions That Have Sold for 23 Years

    A cafe with no address, a menu carrying three questions — how did a book of only 35,701 words take number 9 on Eslite’s 2026 annual bestseller list?

  • Classics & Ideas · About 14 min read

    The Diamond Sutra in Plain Words: How a 1,158-Year-Old Text Made the 2026 Bestseller List

    In 17 lessons, Wang Sixun retells the Diamond Sutra as psychology — and lands at number 8 on Eslite's annual chart. What the book gets right, what it leaves out, and why the scroll printed in 868 is still speaking.

  • People & Society · About 9 min read

    The Courage to Be Disliked: Why a Book from 2013 Is Still on the Bestseller Lists in 2026

    In five nights of dialogue, Ichiro Kishimi and Fumitake Koga turn Adlerian psychology into a single line: freedom is being disliked by others. This deep read asks whether their Adler is the real Adler — and whether a book can fairly call every trouble in life a personal choice.

  • Money & Human Nature · About 12 min read

    Just Keep Buying: Even God Couldn't Beat Dollar-Cost Averaging

    Nick Maggiulli's book came out in Britain in 2022 and reached Taiwan in 2023. Its message is a single sentence: stop guessing the timing, and just keep buying. This deep read asks whether lump-sum investing really wins two-thirds of the time, why even God couldn't beat dollar-cost averaging, and whether an answer computed from a century of American market data still holds in Taiwan.

  • People & Society · About 12 min read

    Life Has Only Two Things: A 2019 Book, Retitled, Reaches Eslite's Top 10 in 2026

    A book from 2019, given a new title and reissued in October 2025, reached No. 10 on Eslite's 2026 annual bestseller chart. Its whole argument fits in one sentence: life has only two things — none of your business, and none of my business. This deep read asks why that sentence lands so hard in 2026, whether it is the same idea as Adler's separation of tasks, and how a book with no method sells at all.

  • Classics & Ideas · About 10 min read

    The Secret of Secrets: A Novel Eight Years in the Making Bets on Where We Go When We Die

    Dan Brown's sixth Robert Langdon novel, eight years after the last, was published in the United States in September 2025 and reached Taiwan in March 2026. This time Langdon is not chasing an ancient artefact in Prague, but a secret about human consciousness and what happens after death. This deep read asks how much of the book's science is real, where the Golem comes from, and why the reviews are so divided.

  • Taiwan · About 11 min read

    Sunlight in Bloom: One Hundred Taiwanese Words That Name Light, Home, and Farewell

    Wen Jo-chiao's collection, published by China Times on January 6, 2026: 100 Taiwanese words, written into 100 poems. It went viral on Threads, reached the top 30 of the 2026 first-half bestseller lists, and was read aloud by the President on International Mother Language Day. This deep read asks: how did a language dismissed as vulgar produce poetry this elegant — and after reading it, will we start speaking Taiwanese?

  • Classics & Ideas · About 10 min read

    Kokuhō: The Gangster's Son Who Became Japan's Living National Treasure

    Shuichi Yoshida's career-staking 20th-anniversary novel: serialised in the Asahi Shimbun for a year and five months, published in two volumes in September 2018, winner of two literary prizes in 2019. The 2025 film broke Japan's live-action box-office record, and the novel flowed back onto the bestseller lists. This deep read asks: how can a novel about kabuki keep readers who know nothing about kabuki turning pages — and between bloodline and talent, who decides?

  • Classics & Ideas · About 11 min read

    Poor Charlie's Almanack: The 99-Year-Old Who Left Behind a Way of Thinking, Not a Fortune

    Edited by Peter D. Kaufman, first published in 2005 and reissued by Stripe Press in 2023; the Traditional Chinese commemorative edition came from Business Weekly in 2024. It reached No. 29 on Kingstone's bestseller chart for the first half of 2026. This deep read asks: how does a collection of speeches stay in print for 20 years? What is a latticework of mental models? And what, exactly, is so 'common' about Munger's common sense?

  • Science & the World · About 11 min read

    Project Hail Mary: A Teacher with Amnesia, an Alien Spider, and a Dying Sun

    Andy Weir's third novel, after The Martian: published by Ballantine on May 4, 2021, voted Goodreads' Best Science Fiction of 2021, and on the New York Times bestseller list for 57 weeks in total by July 2026. The film opened in March 2026 and sent the novel back onto the charts — number 40 on Kingstone's bestseller chart for the first half of 2026 in Taiwan. This deep read asks: how does hard science fiction keep readers who never read science fiction turning pages? And when one person cannot save the world, where does cooperation begin?

  • Classics & Ideas · About 11 min read

    I May Be Wrong: From CFO to Forest Monk, a Life in One Sentence

    The Swedish original went straight to number one on publication in 2020. The Traditional Chinese edition, translated by Guo Tengjian, followed on February 1, 2023, from Xianjue Publishing, and took number one on Books.com.tw's 2024 paperback and e-book charts and number 9 on Kingstone's 2024 Top 100 bestseller list. The author, Björn Natthiko Lindeblad, died on January 17, 2022, aged 60. This deep read asks: why would a man walk away from everything to spend nearly 17 years as a monk in a Thai forest? How did a single sentence carry a book to the top of the charts in Sweden and Taiwan? And what can that sentence do for the rest of us, who are anxious and certain in equal measure?

  • People & Society · About 17 min read

    Eagle-Style Parenting: A Former Pilot on Teaching Children to Fly on Their Own in the AI Era

    Hiram Kang, published by Cite (Fangzhi) on May 1, 2026, 272 pages; number 6 on Kingstone's new-books bestseller chart for the first half of 2026. A former commercial pilot, born in New Zealand and raised by a single mother, turns his mother's 80/20 bet and the cockpit's golden rules into a parenting book. This deep read asks: why did it take off? And is its promise — a life AI cannot replace — a method, or a dose of relief?

  • People & Society · About 14 min read

    Nordic Time: Happiness Is Not Faster, It Is Just Right

    Written by Inko Higurashi and translated by Lin Mei-chi, published by Happiness Culture on October 29, 2025, 224 pages, NT$398; the Japanese original came out from Daiwa Publishing in October 2023, 185 pages. The Traditional Chinese edition reached No. 4 on Eslite's 2026 annual bestseller chart. This deep read asks: what exactly is it selling, that readers in Taiwan are buying? Is the Nordic time in its pages a real life, or a carefully chosen postcard? And if we bring that way of time back to Taiwan, who pays the bill?

  • People & Society · About 16 min read

    My Gangster Aunt: Why a Life Lived Wrong Taught Taiwan to Let Go

    Huang Shan-Liao, <i>My Gangster Aunt: 99 Things She Taught Me</i>, Sun Color Publishing, May 29, 2026, 296 pp.; No. 3 on Kingstone's 2026 Autumn Literature Chart and No. 1 on its Modern Chinese Writing live chart. A woman who left home at 17, went to prison twice, and — by one account — died of liver cancer at 45, left behind 99 maxims. This deep read asks: why her, why now, and what does "Don't live right. Live free" actually cost?

  • Classics & Ideas · About 11 min read

    Sunrise on the Reaping: What Can Still Be Written When Everyone Already Knows the Ending

    Suzanne Collins, published by Scholastic on March 18, 2025, 400 pages; more than 1.5 million English-language copies sold in its first week, number 1 on bestseller lists in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. The Traditional Chinese edition, translated by Wang Xinying, was published by Locus Publishing on June 27, 2026, 512 pages. This deep read asks: how did a story whose ending was written 17 years ago become the bestselling young adult novel of 2025?

  • Classics & Ideas · About 15 min read

    The Miracles of the Namiya General Store: Why a Shop Closed for 33 Years Is Still Answering Letters

    Keigo Higashino, published by Kadokawa Shoten on March 28, 2012; the Traditional Chinese edition, translated by Wang Yun-jie, came out from Crown Culture on August 5, 2013, 352 pages. Winner of the 7th Chuokoron Prize, it passed 10 million copies worldwide by 2017, and Crown said in July 2026 that 14 million people had read it, with 500,000 copies sold in Taiwan. This deep read asks: why did five letters stay in print for 14 years? Is the shop's kindness an answer, or a mirror?

  • Taiwan · About 11 min read

    Jie Jin: Seven Small Knives Cutting Open the Gilded Cracks of Everyday Life

    Huang Li-chun's first story collection in 14 years, published by Current Publishing on October 20, 2026: 192 pages, 7 short stories, from a wedding banquet tasting to the boredom of a ghost. It reached No. 3 on Kingstone's modern Chinese fiction chart and No. 30 on Eslite's October online bestsellers. This deep read asks: why does a novel that refuses to offer a theme prove so hard to put down?

  • Classics & Ideas · About 16 min read

    The Lion's Snack: On the Last Mile of Life, Order the One Treat You Want to Taste Again

    Ito Ogawa's 2019 novel took second place in the 2020 Japan Booksellers' Award, became an 8-episode NHK drama in 2021, and was published in Taiwan on July 5, 2021, translated by Wang Yun-jie; in 2026 it reached number 10 on Books.com.tw's literary fiction chart. Shizuku Umino, 33, has been told her cancer is terminal, and moves into the Lion's House, a hospice on a lemon island in the Seto Inland Sea, where every Sunday at 3 p.m. residents may order the one snack they most want to taste again. This deep read asks: why does a novel about dying make readers want to live more carefully — and has its gentleness made death look too clean?

  • Classics & Ideas · About 12 min read

    Journey Under the Midnight Sun: Nineteen Years in a Night Without a Sun

    Keigo Higashino's 1999 mystery novel, published by Shueisha on August 5, 1999. After the author's death on July 23, 2026, it returned to the bestseller lists — No. 3 on Books.com.tw's e-book chart and a place on Readmoo's 2026 annual bestseller list. A 1973 pawnshop murder in Osaka binds Ryoji and Yukiho together for 19 years. This deep read asks: why does a novel that refuses to explain its protagonists' inner lives still hold readers 27 years later?

  • Taiwan · About 15 min read

    Rebirth: Press "Play," and the Dead Start Talking

    Liu Tzu-chieh's first story collection in 12 years, published by Crown on May 4, 2026: 224 pages, 9 stories, from an antique remote control in a hot-spring inn in Atami to temples, funerals, and city life. On November 2, 2026, it won the Golden Tripod Grand Prize at the Taiwan Literature Awards, taking home NT$1 million; by late November it reached No. 10 on Kingstone's modern Chinese fiction chart. This deep read asks: why does a book about death make you laugh — and why, after the laughter, can't you laugh anymore?

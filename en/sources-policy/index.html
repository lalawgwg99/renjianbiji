Sources & Verification Policy

Where the source list at the end of each article comes from.

Source lists

Every article ends with a list of the main sources checked during writing, including primary documents, academic works, encyclopaedias and institutional sources; each item carries a grade from A to D.

Source grades

Key figures and core claims favour grade A and B sources; Wikipedia is only a starting point for verification, never the sole source. Source lists lead with grade A and B sources, and Wikipedia and other grade D sources appear at the end. The grading standard is in the table below.

Verification process

Core facts are checked by search and cross-referenced against other sources while writing. When figures differ, the differing accounts are presented or noted; matters without a reliable answer are stated as such in the text.

Images

Article images are real photographs or artworks from openly licensed sources such as Wikimedia Commons, each checked by a human editor for relevance and for the absence of watermarks. Licence status is noted in captions or source records.

Limitations

For some earlier articles, the source list gives source names without per-item links; this is noted at the end of those articles. We are gradually completing them.

Principle: key figures and core claims favour grade A and B sources; where one source item mixes several sources, it takes the lowest grade among them. Source lists lead with grade A and B sources, and Wikipedia and other grade D sources appear at the end. A grade describes the nature of the source, not whether the item is necessarily wrong — figures resting only on grade D sources should be cross-checked against other sources.
